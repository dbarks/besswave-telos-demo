# Operational Mission — Sarah Chen

> The 2–4 strategic priorities that frame my work in 2026. These are the lenses through which I make decisions and allocate attention.

## Priority 1: Service Desk Performance

IT is a shared service. When the service desk is slow, every other team's productivity degrades. My top operational priority is getting ticket resolution SLAs to a level the business actually respects — below 2.5 days on average, with P1 incidents resolved same day. The current 4.2-day average is a drag on every team that depends on IT.

## Priority 2: Operational Capacity Alignment

MidWest Distribution is growing its sales pipeline faster than operations is scaling to absorb it. I need visibility into whether our current facilities, staffing, and systems can handle a conversion surge from the pipeline. When Sales closes a large account, Operations has about 3 weeks to prepare. I need to know, in advance, when we're approaching the edge of what we can absorb.

## Priority 3: Infrastructure Modernization

Two of our three sites are running on aging device hardware and patchy network infrastructure. The Chicago device refresh is the immediate priority — 85 devices, most over 4 years old, creating a disproportionate share of our Intune compliance failures and IT tickets. The goal is to clear Chicago by end of Q2 and assess Columbus for Q4.

## Priority 4: Cost Discipline Without Headcount Reduction

I need to find 8% operational savings year-over-year without cutting people. The lever is automation, self-service, and eliminating low-value manual work (like ticket triaging, spreadsheet maintenance, and manual data pulls). Besswave is part of this — if the time my team spends gathering information decreases, that capacity goes toward higher-value work.
